# Historicising Speculative Design: codebook and archives

A prototype for Study 1 of the PhD project *Historicising Speculative Design* (Soyoun Jang, Georgia Tech).

The archive collects claims that a new medium would transform or replace the book in education, from the classroom stereoscope and educational film to today's discourse on VR and AI. Each claim is coded on eight codes:

| Code | Source | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Tetrad law(s) | McLuhan & McLuhan 1988 | enhance, obsolesce, retrieve, reverse |
| Scope of change | specific to obsolescence | how far a speculation departs from its present, by what it leaves behind: nothing, a practice, a medium, an institution |
| Temporal distance | construal-level theory | stated horizon in five-year steps: 0–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20, 20+ years, unstated; years recorded where given |
| Spatial distance | construal-level theory | distance of the setting from the speaker: here, elsewhere; unspecified kept off the scale |
| Social distance | construal-level theory | us, others (incl. future generations and non-humans); unspecified kept off the scale |
| Hypotheticality | construal-level theory | will, should, could, is (depicted) |
| Construal level | construal-level theory (outcome) | concrete or abstract language |
| Format | | press or magazine, talk or broadcast, book, academic paper, report, novel or story, film or television, image, design project |
| Direction | | forward (a future), backward (a counterfactual past), sideways (a parallel present) |
| Medium | | stereograph, film, radio, television, teaching machine, computer, virtual reality, internet, AI, direct transmission to the brain (source wording kept as a detail) |
| Medium status | | whether the medium, as described in its educational use, existed at the date: existing (in use beyond its makers), emerging (only its makers' prototype, trial or demonstration), imagined (no working version, even if its parts existed) |
| Speaker | | inventor, company, policymaker, educator, researcher, designer, writer or artist |

*Speculative distance* (how far a claim departs from its own present) is coded on five dimensions: the four dimensions of psychological distance in construal-level theory (Trope & Liberman 2010), as marked in the claim's text, plus scope of change, which the theory lacks because it studies everyday events rather than imagined changes to the world. Psychological distance proper is a person's sense of distance and cannot be read from a document, hence the separate term. Construal level is coded as what distance is expected to produce, so the archive can test whether far claims were in fact written abstractly. Scope of change is the primary dimension: crossed with the Tetrad, it forms the wheel. The others, including temporal distance, are reported descriptively; a horizon belongs to a claim as a whole rather than to any one law, and most claims state none.

Claims of replacement mostly state what a medium enhances and obsolesces, so the archive records only what was predicted. Reading these predictions against what each medium did in practice (television did not replace the book, but it did retrieve the lecture) belongs to the written analysis, drawing on histories of educational technology, not to the archive. The speaker code asks whether a claim's distance follows the interests behind it, and whether speculative design projects sit at different distances from commercial forecasts (Wong & Khovanskaya 2018).

Temporal distance is coded from the horizon a claim *states*, not a year, because most claims (and most speculative design) give none: Edison's "soon", a design fiction's "near future". The signalling phrase is recorded with the code, the horizon in years is recorded where a year or number is given (so the five-year steps can be redrawn without recoding), and *unstated* is a value in its own right: in construal-level terms, a vague horizon may itself indicate abstract construal. Placing phrases on the scale is interpretive, so the codebook gives example phrases per level and a second coder checks a sample.

## Pages

The site opens on an Introduction: what historicising means here (after Soden et al. 2021, reading every speculation, speculative design included, as a document of its own present), what the project aims to do (code what speculations imagine and leave out, through the Tetrad and speculative distance) and what the two cases are for (Case 1, the book, for depth; Case 2, climate, for breadth). Then the Codebook. Case 1 (Past futures of the book) has two sub-tabs: Tetrad wheel and Sources. On the wheel, hovering a speculation that codes for several laws previews the lines linking its copies; selecting it keeps them. Case 2 (Climate futures) is not on the site yet; its corpus plan is kept in the vault. Readings and design are kept off the site; they are developed privately for publication.

- **Archive** — the Tetrad wheel: four sectors for the Tetrad laws, with rings for **scope of change**. Hover a dot for a summary; select it and the overview card flips to a grey depth-2 card with its full coding ("← Overview" or selecting again flips back). Dots are coloured by media family and filled for concrete language or hollow for abstract. Selecting a speculation that spans several laws links and pulses all its copies. Hatched cells are empty: combinations no speculation codes for; selecting one shows a short note. The overview shows the counts as a Tetrad figure, with the four laws around the number of speculations. Below it, counts the wheel cannot show: media family, medium status, stated horizon (imminent, near, distant, unstated) and speaker. Media-family chips (stereograph and film, radio and television, teaching machines, computers, internet, AI, VR and direct transmission) select what to show (all media by default; click a family to show only it, click it again to return to all media); a decade slider ("Play decades") handles time.
- **Codebook** — the full coding scheme (v0.1): unit of analysis, attributes (recorded), codes (interpreted: Tetrad, speculative distance, construal level) with definitions, decision rules and examples from the archive, a worked example (Edison 1922 coded on every dimension, with reasoning), and the reliability plan. The table below summarises it.
- **Sources** — a timeline of every speculation by year above a list pane that scrolls on its own. Hover a dot for a summary; select it to bring its source to the top of the list. Search filters both. How the corpus was built is kept in the vault (Case 1 - Book/Corpus), not on the site.

## Status of the data

**Pilot sample of 31 documented speculations** (1906–2025): Edison (1911, 1913, 1922, 1925), the Keystone teachers' guide (1906), Villemard's *En l'an 2000* school card (c.1910), the Ohio School of the Air (1929), Asimov's "The Fun They Had" (1951), Bradbury's *Fahrenheit 451* (1953), FCC Commissioner Frieda Hennock (1955), Skinner (1958), RCA's *Electronic Age* (1959), Senator Muskie (1961), *The Jetsons*, "Elroy's Mob" (1963), Suppes (1966), Illich's *Deschooling Society* (1971), Papert (1984), Apple's *Knowledge Navigator* (1987), Winn (1993), Stephenson's *The Diamond Age* (1995), *The Matrix* (1999), Thrun (2012), *Star Trek* (2009), Corning's *A Day Made of Glass 2* (2012), Christensen (2013), Bayne's Teacherbot (2015), Selwyn et al.'s "school of 2030" (2020), the OECD's "Learn-as-you-go" scenario (2020), Sal Khan (2023) and Bill Gates (2023, 2025).

Each records its **format**, the form in which it was first made: press or magazine, talk or broadcast, book, academic paper, report, novel or story, film or television, image, or design project. Format is descriptive; whether a work functions as speculative design is a question for the analysis, not a code.

Codes are provisional (one coder, codebook v0.1). Entries marked *to verify* need checking against the original page, edition or venue. Still missing: a documented 1960s claim that television would replace the book, 1990s claims that VR would replace the classroom, and more speculative design projects.

The data lives in `data/book.json` and `data/climate.json` (see Data below).

To add a page: create `pages/<name>.html` with a `<section id="pg-<name>" class="page" hidden>`, add its name to `PAGE_FILES` in `index.html`, and add it to `PAGES` and `SECTIONS` in `js/app.js`.

## Running it

The site loads its pages and data, so serve the folder rather than opening `index.html` directly: run `python3 -m http.server` here and visit `http://localhost:8000` (or use VS Code's Live Server extension). Fonts load from Google Fonts.

## Data

The coding and the sources live outside this repository and stay private; the repository holds only what is published:

- **Coding sheet** (Google Sheets, private): one row per speculation, one tab per case, every codebook attribute in its own column, with dropdowns drawn from the codebook values. This is the source of truth for the coding. Coding notes and to-verify notes stay in the sheet: the export publishes only each speculation's quotation, codes and citation.
- **Source library** (private Obsidian vault): one note per work, named `Lastname Year - Short Title`, holding the citation, coding notes and the speculations it contains, with the PDFs, threads and readings. The sheet's "Source note" column gives each row's note. Readings are never published.

The site reads exported copies in `data/`:

- `data/book.json` and `data/book-repeats.json` — Case 1, past futures of the book: counted speculations, and repeats set aside under one per speaker per medium. Rows marked To check in the sheet are not exported.
- `data/climate.json` — Case 2, climate futures (empty for now).

To update the site after editing the sheet, download the "Case 1: Book" tab as CSV and run `python3 tools/sheet_to_json.py path/to/file.csv`. The script rebuilds both Case 1 files, keeps their headers and value lists, and stops with a message if a row uses a value the codebook does not list or has no Tetrad law. A Tetrad law counts as held when its column (Enhances, Obsolesces, Retrieves, Reverses into) has text in it.

Because the site loads these files, open it through a web server rather than as a local file, e.g. `python3 -m http.server` in this folder, then visit `http://localhost:8000`.


## Codebook v0.2

One codebook for both cases; what each case collects, and its media list, sits with its corpus (Case 1 › Sources; Case 2). Labels were generalised for both cases (scope: replaces a medium / an institution; social: us, others, everyone). Spatial distance now follows construal-level theory strictly, as distance from the speaker (here, elsewhere, everywhere); reach is recorded as an attribute. `data/book.json` was migrated to these labels (v0.2); Star Trek (2009) was recoded as elsewhere.

Attributes are grouped as *the record* (year, attribution, speaker, format, text, source with a to-verify flag, notes) and *what it imagines* (direction, medium, medium status, reach); medium status and reach need decision rules and are in the reliability check. New rules: researcher vs speculative designer; the central medium when several are involved; medium status for backward speculation judged at the point of divergence.

## Codebook v0.3 and corpora

Reach was removed: it overlapped with spatial distance and matters little for climate, which is mostly global. "Speculative designer" is now "Designer", with rules separating designers, writers or artists and researchers. Construal is coded from the work itself, never from the archive's one-line description. Installations, games and live performances are excluded for now and reported as a limitation.

Each project has a **Corpus** sub-tab beside the wheel stating scope and how the corpus was developed. Case 1's pilot was assembled purposively (with live counts by format and period); its CHI and DIS extension will state its search procedure. Case 2 takes the 28 project papers from Soden et al. (2021b): 40 of 171 ACM papers (2009–2021) had sustainability as the primary issue and speculative design or a related practice as the primary method; 28 of these are project papers.

## Codebook v0.5 and the first extension

The ACM Digital Library extension added 15 speculations from 7 papers (ids 32–46). Media gained *robot* (shown with AI in an "AI and robots" family) and *game* (a "Games" family, after McLuhan 1964). Speaker gained *Workshop participants*, for speculations made by the people a study engages rather than by its authors. One speculation per speaker per medium counts in a case: when a speaker repeats a view about the same medium across works, the earliest counts; a speculation about a different medium counts separately, and distinct speculations within one work all count. The repeats (Edison 1913, 1922 and 1925; Gates 2010 and 2025; Papert 1984, 1996, 1997 and 2006) are kept in `data/book-repeats.json` and kept out of every count. The archive now counts 43: 28 from the pilot and 15 from the extension.

Later in v0.5, temporal distance moved to five-year steps (0–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20, 20+ years, unstated), with a **Horizon (years)** column for the stated horizon wherever a year or number is given (`horizon_years` in the data); a range is coded by its upper bound. The unit of analysis now reads "imagines the world otherwise than its present: changed by a medium or, where no medium is named, simply changed". Medium keeps McLuhan's broad sense, glossed for HCI as the sociotechnical intervention imagined (a technology, artefact, practice or policy), and may be *N/A* when a speculation names no medium at all: such speculations are coded on distance and construal but not on medium status or the Tetrad, and are kept off the wheel and counted separately.


Scope of change was then redefined as departure from the speculation's own present, read from what it leaves behind, the object of *Obsolesces*: leaves nothing behind, a practice, a medium, or an institution (replacing supplements, transforms practice, replaces a medium, replaces an institution). Scope measures depth, not breadth, within each case's domain (Case 1: the school; Case 2: the wider impact of climate change on how people live). Speculations that leave nothing behind share one level whatever their rhetoric; construal and hypotheticality record the difference. Departure is not critique: whether a speculation is critical is left to the analysis.
