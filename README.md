# An Archive of Past Futures

A prototype view for Study 1 of the PhD project *Historicising Speculative Design* (Soyoun Jang, Georgia Tech).

The archive collects claims that a new medium would transform or replace the book in education, from the classroom stereoscope and educational film to today's discourse on VR and AI. Each claim is coded on two dimensions:

- **McLuhan's Tetrad** (McLuhan & McLuhan 1988): what the claim promised to *enhance*, *obsolesce*, *retrieve* and *reverse*.
- **Speculative distance** from the claim's own present, drawing on construal-level theory (Trope & Liberman 2010): scope of change (supplements, transforms practice, replaces the medium, replaces the institution), time horizon, and concrete or abstract language.

## Views

- **Tetrad wheel** — four sectors for the Tetrad laws, rings for speculative distance. Hatched cells are *gaps*: combinations no claim imagined. Opening a gap shows the design fiction it calls for, with a multiplane animation sketch.
- **Timeline** — each claim drawn from the year it was made to the year it predicted, ending by outcome (absorbed, not realised, still open).
- A decade slider ("Play decades") and era filters.

## Status of the data

**Mock-up.** All entries are illustrative paraphrases, except Edison's 1913 statement ("Books will soon be obsolete in the public schools"), which is a documented claim still to be verified against its source (commonly cited via Cuban 1986). Replace the `CLAIMS` array in `index.html` with sourced entries as the archive is collected.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. It is a single self-contained page; fonts load from Google Fonts.
