Scope

Speculations that a medium will transform or replace the book, reading, teaching or the school, from 1906 to the present, in any format. Excluded: speculations about media with no bearing on learning or reading, and works that cannot be quoted or described from a document, such as installations, games and live performances.

Media: stereograph · film · radio · television · teaching machine · computer · virtual reality · internet · AI · direct transmission to the brain. For colour, they are shown in seven families: stereograph and film · radio and television · teaching machines · computers · internet · AI · VR and direct transmission.

How it was developed

Pilot (31 speculations). Assembled purposively, not by systematic search: claims quoted in histories of educational technology (Cuban 1986; Watters 2021) and traced, where possible, to the press, broadcast and archival sources they cite; widely discussed fiction, film and images; and scenario and speculative design work on education (e.g. Bayne 2015; Selwyn et al. 2020; OECD 2020). Each claim records its source, and a to verify flag where the wording comes only from a secondary source.

Extension (in progress). Speculative design projects from CHI and DIS on AI tutors, future classrooms and similar themes. The search procedure, venues, years, terms and inclusion criteria, will be stated here before they are coded.

Limitation. A purposive pilot leans towards well-known, mostly American sources; the extension and its stated search procedure are meant to correct this.

References

  • Larry Cuban. 1986. Teachers and Machines: The Classroom Use of Technology Since 1920. Teachers College Press.
  • Audrey Watters. 2021. Teaching Machines: The History of Personalized Learning. MIT Press.
