Introduction

Every new medium has been promised as the one that would replace the book in education, from Edison’s films in 1913 to AI tutors today; the climate has been imagined for even longer, from ecological fiction of the 1880s to net-zero scenarios. Such futures shaped what was funded and built. Many of them are now past for us, which makes it possible to read them against what actually happened.

Speculative design imagines futures too, not to predict them but to question the present (Dunne & Raby 2013). Yet it is said to resemble the corporate and policy futures it sets out to critique (Wong & Khovanskaya 2018). And it comes late to a much longer tradition of imagining futures, in futures studies, science fiction and earlier design movements. How does speculative design imagine differently from these, and where does it imagine the same? Recent speculative design literature offers evaluative frames (Baumer et al. 2020), lists of qualities (Ringfort-Felner et al. 2025) and reviews of papers (Soden et al. 2021b; Sanchez et al. 2025), but reads speculative design on its own, not alongside these other futures.

In a sense, the project speculates about speculative design itself. I propose a framework for reading a speculation (a codebook), use it to identify patterns in how futures have been imagined, and from these, find ways to evaluate speculative design and imagine it otherwise, through a set of design prompts that I hope eventually to make into games. The cases are bounded corpora, so the patterns they show are claims about these cases, to be tested elsewhere.

Approach

Each speculation is read in two ways. McLuhan’s Tetrad asks what it imagines a new medium will enhance, obsolesce, retrieve and reverse. Speculative distance asks how far it departs from its own present: the scope of change it claims, plus the four dimensions of psychological distance in construal-level theory (Trope & Liberman 2010). Construal level is coded separately, to test the theory’s prediction that distant futures are construed abstractly.

This is historicising in Soden et al.’s (2021a) sense: every speculation, speculative design included, is read as a document of its own present. Edison’s 1913 claim is measured from 1913, not from today.

Two cases

Equal in weight, read separately, then compared. Together they span academic papers, reports, science fiction, design projects, film and images, from the 1900s to the present.
